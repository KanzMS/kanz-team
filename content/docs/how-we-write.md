## Character

Premium editorial finance: a trading terminal crossed with a serious financial journal. Calm, precise and never playful.

Because the product is financial, being accurate comes before sounding good. Every number says what it measures and whose number it is. A missing figure is better than a wrong one.

## Our analysis is never a trade

Kanz's analysis is technical analysis for education. Copy about it describes the chart and never names an action.

| Never, about our analysis | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Stop, stop loss | Invalidation level («مستوى إبطال النموذج») |
| Target of the desk | Pattern target («هدف النموذج») |
| Signal, pick, call, opportunity | Pattern, chart pattern («النموذج السعري») |

**Banned in English:** trade, trading, buy, sell, hold, long, short, signal, entry, exit, opportunity, pick and call (as a recommendation).

**Banned in Arabic:** صفقة، تداول، شراء، بيع، احتفاظ، إشارة، فرصة، توصية

Three things may keep those words:

- **The disclaimer**, which names what the analysis is not.
- **A member's own transactions**, in their portfolio and tools.
- **Standard market terms**, such as «حجم التداول» (volume).

Someone else's ratings appear only under a heading that says "Third-party".

## Name the method

Our method is the **Classical Charting Method** («منهج التحليل الفني الكلاسيكي»), capitalised as a proper name in English. What the analysts identify is a **pattern** or **chart pattern**. "Setup" appears only where it is the interface's own label, paired with "pattern" on first use.

Flags are the analysts' classification, set by hand. Never say Kanz adds or removes one automatically.

## Style rules

- No em-dashes in English. Use a colon, a comma or two sentences.
- Plain words over jargon.
- Name a thing the way the product names it: the bookmarks list is always **Watchlist**.
- Arabic that needs judgement is written by the team, never machine-generated.
